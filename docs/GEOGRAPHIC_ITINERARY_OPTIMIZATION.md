# Systematic Geographic Itinerary Optimization

## 1. Overview & Architectural Goals
The objective of the Geographic Itinerary Optimization engine is to guarantee that multi-day itineraries generated by Namma Karnataka (ನಮ್ಮ ಕರ್ನಾಟಕ) form a physically continuous, geographically coherent circuit rather than disconnected, random-feeling leaps across Karnataka.

Historically, itineraries could pair Mysuru (South) on Day 1 with Gokarna/Karwar (Coastal North) on Day 2 and Hampi (Central-East) on Day 3, causing daily driving distances of 500+ km and 7–8 hours of behind-the-wheel fatigue. The Phase 10+ architecture solves this through:
1. **Regional Corridor Clustering:** Grouping Karnataka's 31 districts into 6 cohesive geographic corridors.
2. **Deterministic Corridor Selection & Distant District Transparency:** Ranking corridors against user preferences and explaining why distant districts are omitted.
3. **Sequential 2-Opt & Nearest-Neighbor Route Optimization:** Organizing candidate stops per day into continuous circuits starting from the trip origin on Day 1 and successive overnight hotels on subsequent days.
4. **Deterministic Tie-Breaking:** Ensuring reproducible, stable itineraries given fixed inputs.

## 3. Corridor Selection & Scoring Model
When a user submits trip preferences (origin, selected districts, category preferences, duration), the engine evaluates candidate corridors using the following formula:

$$\text{Corridor Score} = W_{\text{dist}} \cdot S_{\text{dist}} + W_{\text{cat}} \cdot S_{\text{cat}} - W_{\text{pen}} \cdot P_{\text{distance}}$$

Where:
- $S_{\text{dist}}$: Proportion of user-selected districts covered by the corridor.
- $S_{\text{cat}}$: Compatibility between the corridor's iconic destination categories (e.g. `heritage`, `wildlife`, `beach`) and the user's category preferences.
- $P_{\text{distance}}$: Distance penalty from the traveler's starting hub coordinates to the corridor centroid.
- **Distant District Transparency:** If the user selected districts that fall outside the winning corridor (e.g., selecting Bidar while touring Mysuru in a 2-day trip), the engine explicitly identifies these districts and generates a localized bilingual warning:
  - *English:* "Districts outside this corridor (Bidar) were omitted to maintain realistic travel times within 2 days."
  - *Kannada:* "ವಾಸ್ತವಿಕ ಪ್ರಯಾಣ ಸಮಯವನ್ನು ಕಾಯ್ದುಕೊಳ್ಳಲು ಈ ಕಾರಿಡಾರ್‌ನ ಹೊರಗಿನ ಜಿಲ್ಲೆಗಳನ್ನು (Bidar) ಕೈಬಿಡಲಾಗಿದೆ."

## 4. Candidate Stop Selection & Day Allocation
Within the chosen corridor:
1. **Hard Requirements:** User-selected required destinations are prioritized and placed into the candidate pool first.
2. **Catalogue Filtering:** Only verified destinations with non-null coordinates, valid operating status, and reasonable visit durations are considered.
3. **Preference Ranking:** Candidates are scored on:
   - Category match (+3 points).
   - Offbeat preference match (+2 points).
   - District match (+2 points).
   - Budget feasibility (+1 point).
4. **Day Capacity:** Sightseeing stops are capped at 3–4 major attractions per day (accounting for 1.5–3 hours per stop plus lunch and transit) to prevent overloaded schedules.

---

## 5. Route Optimization Engine (`RouteOptimizer`)
Destination ordering uses a sequential nearest-neighbor construction with local 2-opt improvements:
1. **Day 1 Start:** The route begins at the traveler's resolved origin hub (e.g., Bengaluru coordinates: 12.9716° N, 77.5946° E).
2. **Sequential Selection:** The stop that minimizes driving transit from the current location is chosen next, subject to operating hours.
3. **2-Opt Leg Optimization:** For a daily sequence $[S_0, S_1, \dots, S_n]$, if reversing sub-segment $(S_i, S_j)$ decreases total road distance without violating opening hours, the reversal is accepted:
   $$\Delta D = (d(S_{i-1}, S_j) + d(S_i, S_{j+1})) - (d(S_{i-1}, S_i) + d(S_j, S_{j+1})) < 0$$
4. **End-of-Day Stay Placement:** The final destination of Day $d$ anchors the overnight accommodation hub.
5. **Overnight Continuity:** Day $d+1$ begins directly from the coordinates of the Day $d$ accommodation, ensuring no teleportation or artificial resets to the original origin.
6. **Return Leg:** If `is_return_trip=True`, a dedicated `StopType.TRANSPORT` is appended to Day $N$, returning to the original starting location.

---

## 6. Deterministic Tie-Breaking
When two candidate routes or stops have identical scores or distances:
1. Lower estimated driving time takes precedence.
2. Higher destination rating / verified completeness takes precedence.
3. Lexicographical comparison of destination unique `id` ensures 100% deterministic, reproducible output across runs.
